The problem with passive watching

Watching charts for hours feels like practice. It isn't. You're not building pattern recognition — you're building hindsight rationalisation. Every move looks obvious after it happens.

Real-time paper trading is worse: a daily chart produces one bar a day. To get 200 meaningful reps, you'd need eight months.

What deliberate practice looks like

You need to commit a prediction before the bar reveals. No taking it back. That commitment is what creates a genuine learning signal — your brain has to be right or wrong, not vaguely impressed in hindsight.

With historical replay, you get 200 forced decisions per session. Eight months of daily chart watching compressed into two hours.

Questions

Is it free?
Yes. The trainer is free with no sign-up required. Creating an account lets you save session progress across devices — predictions, trades, notes, and your position in the dataset.
How is this different from paper trading?
Paper trading in real time means waiting for bars to form — 6.5 hours a day on a daily chart. With historical replay you get 200+ practice reps per session. More importantly, the prediction system forces you to commit before the outcome, which eliminates the hindsight bias that makes passive chart-watching almost useless for building skill.
What is blind mode?
Blind mode hides the ticker symbol. You can't use prior knowledge about a stock's history to bias your read. You're forced to read the structure, not the name — which is the actual skill.
What does noise injection do?
It adds small random perturbations to each bar's OHLC values. This prevents you from recognising specific historical sequences you've already studied, keeping every session novel even on datasets you've used before.
Can I review my trades after a session?
Yes. The session summary shows your full trade log with entry, TP, SL, exit price, R:R, and outcome. Click Review on any trade and the chart jumps back to your entry bar — you step forward through the move bar by bar and see exactly how it played out against your levels.
What is price action trading?
Price action trading means making decisions based solely on how price moves — without lagging indicators like RSI or MACD. You read candlestick patterns, market structure (higher highs and lows, lower highs and lows), support and resistance levels, and the behaviour of wicks at key levels.
